The problem I solve

The product works. Nobody has tested the trust boundaries.

Fast, AI-assisted development ships features quickly and skips the checks that decide whether one customer can reach another's data. That's the work I do, by hand.

Authorization & IDOR/BOLA

Can user A reach user B's data? Can a normal user hit an admin-only function? I test it the tedious way, one role and one object at a time.

Tenant isolation

Multi-tenant products live or die on isolation. Being logged in isn't the same as being allowed, so I check that every read and write is scoped to the right tenant.

APIs & business logic

The endpoints you never documented, workflows run out of order, and fields like role or price that the server quietly trusts from the client.

AI agent & tool boundaries

Prompt injection, and whether the model's output can make a tool do something privileged with no real authorization check. Plus what your retrieval layer hands back to the wrong user.

Authentication & sessions

Login, logout, password reset, token handling, and the moment a user's privileges change. These are the workflows an attacker reaches first.

Developer-ready remediation

I come from a development background, so each finding is written for the engineer who has to fix it. You get the change to make, not a severity label and good luck.

Straight answers

Questions I usually get

We already use a scanner. Why do we need this?

Scanners are useful, but they don't understand your workflows or your authorization model. A scanner won't tell you that tenant A can read tenant B's records, or that your AI assistant can cancel a booking it shouldn't. That's the part I test.

We're a small team. Aren't we too small for this?

Small teams are where this pays off most. Fixing an authorization bug before launch is far cheaper than explaining a data breach to your customers after.

Can you guarantee we have no vulnerabilities?

No, and anyone who promises that is lying to you. A time-boxed review covers the areas we agreed to test, and the report is clear about what I didn't get to.

What do I get at the end?

A report an engineer can act on. Each finding has reproduction steps, the impact, the root cause, and the fix. After you've patched things, I retest once.

Is this legal / authorized?

Only with written scope and authorization confirming you're allowed to permit testing of the systems listed. I don't go outside it.
