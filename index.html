The problem I solve

The product works. Nobody has tested the trust boundaries.

Fast, AI-assisted development ships features quickly and quietly skips the checks that decide whether one customer can reach another customer's data. That's the work I do — by hand.

Authorization & IDOR/BOLA

Can user A reach user B's data? Can tenant A reach tenant B? Can a normal user reach admin functions? Tested role-by-role and object-by-object.

Tenant isolation

Multi-tenant products live or die on isolation. I verify that every object read and write is scoped to the caller's tenant — not just authenticated.

APIs & business logic

Hidden endpoints, state-transition abuse, mass assignment, and server-side trust of client-controlled role/price/owner values.

AI agent & tool boundaries

Prompt injection, and whether model output can trigger privileged tool actions without server-side authorization. RAG/retrieval isolation.

Authentication & sessions

Login/logout, password reset and recovery, token handling, and privilege changes — the workflows attackers reach first.

Straight answers

Questions I usually get

We already use a scanner. Why do we need this?

Scanners are useful, but they can't understand your business workflows or your authorization model. A scanner won't tell you that tenant A can read tenant B's records, or that your AI assistant can cancel a booking it shouldn't. That manual trust-boundary testing is exactly what I do.

We're a small team — aren't we too small for this?

Small teams are where a pre-launch review is most economical. It's far cheaper to fix an authorization bug before launch than to explain a data exposure to your customers after.

Can you guarantee we have no vulnerabilities?

No — and anyone who promises that is misleading you. A time-boxed manual review gives scoped assurance over the areas we agree to test. I state the limitations plainly in every report.

What do I get at the end?

A report an engineer can act on: prioritized findings, each with reproduction steps, impact, root cause, and a specific remediation — plus one retest after you fix.

Is this legal / authorized?

Only ever with written scope and authorization confirming you have the authority to permit testing of the listed systems. I don't test anything outside that.
