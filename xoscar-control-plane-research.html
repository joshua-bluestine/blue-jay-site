Xoscar actor control-plane disclosure

I reported unauthenticated access to Xoscar actor control functions. The maintainers documented this related exposure in the public advisory for GHSA-mmvj-m246-m9vf / CVE-2026-49950 and list me, Joshua Bluestine, as a reporter.

Joshua Bluestine · Published October 2026 · Coordinated disclosure

The public record

My report concerned control messages that could reach an exposed actor-pool port without peer authentication. An unauthenticated peer could request configuration and topology information or stop a pool. This is related to the advisory's separate deserialization issue through the same missing peer-authentication boundary. I am a credited reporter on the combined advisory, not the sole reporter of its remote-code-execution finding.

Read the maintainer advisory

Why this mattered

Application-level login does not protect a separate internal actor transport. When an actor port is reachable by an untrusted peer, control operations need their own authentication. The issue illustrates a common assessment question: which internal interfaces quietly assume every network peer is trusted?

Resolution and scope

Xoscar 0.12.0 introduced opt-in actor-transport authentication using XOSCAR_AUTH_KEY. Administrators must configure a strong, nonempty key on every communicating process and restrict actor-port access to intended peers. The advisory states that an unset or empty key retains the legacy unauthenticated mode, so upgrading alone does not enable this protection.

The advisory consolidates multiple reports under one identifier. Its title and critical rating concern the deserialization path; my credited control-plane report should be understood as a related finding, not a claim that I discovered every issue in that advisory.

What this says about my testing approach

I test the trust boundary behind a feature, not only the feature's public interface. For SaaS products, that means checking the APIs and roles behind customer-facing screens. For AI systems, it includes the tools and internal services that a model or agent can call.
