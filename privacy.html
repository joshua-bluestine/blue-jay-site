Trust / Privacy

Privacy & data handling

What this site collects, what happens to your inquiry, and how your app’s test accounts, evidence and report are handled during a review. Short version: as little as possible, kept no longer than needed, and never shared without your say-so.

Last updated 16 November 2026. Blue Jay Application Security is run by Joshua Bluestine in the United States.

On this website

  • No analytics, tracking pixels or advertising. The site loads no third-party scripts, fonts or trackers, and sets no cookies.
  • One setting stays in your browser. If you press “Pause motion”, that choice is kept in your browser’s session storage until you close the tab. It is never sent anywhere.
  • The free checkers run on your device. Rules you paste into the Supabase RLS checker or the Firebase rules checker are analyzed in your browser and never leave the page.
  • Hosting. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may log technical details of requests, such as IP addresses, for security and operations, under GitHub’s privacy statement. I don’t receive or use those logs.

When you get in touch

The inquiry form asks for your email, your app’s URL, what you built and your timing. It is delivered to me through FormSubmit, which processes submissions to deliver them and keeps them under its own policy (currently 30 days). Email, whether sent through the form or directly, is handled in Google Workspace.

I use these details only to reply, scope a review and keep a record of our conversation. I don’t sell them, add you to a mailing list or share them with anyone else. If you’d like your inquiry and our emails deleted, email jbluestine@bluejayappsec.com and I’ll confirm once it’s done, except where I need to keep a record for tax or legal reasons.

During a security review

Every review starts with a written scope and your written authorization. Nothing is tested before both are in place, and only what the scope names is tested.

What I ask for

Two or more test accounts you create for the review, the URL and a description of what your app does. I never ask for your own passwords, production API keys or real customer data, and I’ll tell you to revoke anything sent by mistake.

Real user data

Testing uses the test accounts. If a check unavoidably shows real user data, for example by proving one account can read another’s, I record only the minimum needed to show the issue, mask personal details in the report, and tell you promptly.

Where evidence lives

Requests, responses, screenshots and notes stay on my encrypted work devices and in my Google Workspace account, which is protected by two-factor sign-in. They aren’t kept in public repositories, shared drives or AI tools that train on your data.

Who sees the report

Findings go only to you and the people you name. Serious issues are reported to you directly, in private, as soon as they are confirmed, not held for the final report.

How long it’s kept

Raw testing evidence is deleted 90 days after the final recheck. I keep a copy of the final report and our agreement so I can answer later questions, unless you ask me to delete the report too.

Publishing

I never name a client or publish details of their app without written approval. Founding-rate clients agree to an anonymized case study, which they read and approve before anything is published.

After the review, please disable or delete the test accounts you created.

Questions and changes

Questions about this page or your data go to jbluestine@bluejayappsec.com. If these practices change, this page is updated with a new date. The written scope of a review can add stricter handling, such as a shorter retention period, and the scope wins where the two differ.
